Experiment & Initiative

Forest / 留林

A small experiment in how place, attention and creative practice can change what we notice and what we choose to care about.

If the forest disappeared, what would you keep?

如果森林消失，你想留下什么？

The question

How does a place become meaningful enough for us to care about it?

Forest / 留林 begins in Xishuangbanna. It explores the relationship between people and place through repeated encounters with forests and natural spaces, then brings those encounters into an ordinary place where people eat, talk and pass through everyday life.

The practice

A way of seeing

Go there

Return to a real place.

Pay attention

Look longer than usual.

Make something

Turn an encounter into a trace.

Bring it back

Place the trace in everyday life.

Return

See what changes when we look again.

The method is deliberately simple. We do not begin by telling people what to think about the forest. We begin by giving attention somewhere to go.

The field

The forest is the field. Repeated visits create a relationship with place rather than a one-off encounter.

The interface

A small family rice-noodle restaurant in Jinghong becomes the interface between forest experience and everyday life.

The trace

Photography, oil painting and Chinese calligraphy become traces of what was seen, felt and considered worth remembering.

First edition

01 — What Would You Keep?

Two siblings will visit forests and natural spaces around Jinghong. Photography records. Oil painting interprets. Chinese calligraphy distils a question, a word or a memory. The works will remain in the family restaurant as a continuing exhibition and growing archive.

The project does not speak for conservation science. It creates a cultural and spatial interface where scientific knowledge, local memory, personal attention and everyday encounters can meet.

The archive

What begins as a few works can become a record of place.

Each encounter can leave a trace. Over time, these traces can form an archive of forests, memories, observations and responses. Future editions may include new field sites, artists, community contributions, exhibitions, collectors and conservation partners.

Invitation

Look again.

You do not need to buy anything or know anything about art. You can simply stop, look and leave a trace of what you noticed. If this place matters to you, there may be something you want to keep, make, share or carry back with you.
